# SurakshyaPath Analysis

## 1. Main observed pattern

The clearest pattern is a separation between the severity-only model and the other three models.

On the canonical dataset, frequency-only, frequency-severity, and current-severity-recency produce the same zone ordering. Severity-only produces a different ordering, with Thamel ranked first rather than Bouddha.

This difference is also visible downstream: the severity-only model changes six of ten zone allocations under the 12-officer budget, with a total absolute officer change of 10.

## 2. Why the models can diverge

The models measure different properties of the same synthetic records.

- Frequency-only emphasizes how many incidents occur in a zone.
- Severity-only emphasizes the average severity of incidents in a zone.
- Frequency-severity uses the sum of severity, combining incident count and severity.
- Current-severity-recency additionally reduces the contribution of older incidents.

Because severity-only uses an average, a zone with fewer but relatively severe incidents can move upward even when its total incident volume is lower. The other models retain a stronger relationship with the number of records.

This is an interpretation of the implemented formulas, not a claim about real-world crime behavior.

## 3. Robustness across seeds

The five-seed experiment provides a useful stability check.

Frequency-only selected Bouddha as the top zone in all five scenarios because it is the reference model and its generated spatial counts remained concentrated there under the current generator.

Frequency-severity also selected Bouddha in all five scenarios and had mean absolute rank shifts of only 0.0–0.2 positions relative to frequency-only.

Current-severity-recency likewise selected Bouddha in all five scenarios and had rank shifts of 0.0–0.2. One scenario changed two zone allocations relative to frequency-only.

Severity-only was more variable: Thamel was top-ranked in four scenarios, while Chabahil became top-ranked in one. Its mean absolute rank shift remained between 1.8 and 2.8 positions, and it changed 6–7 zone allocations relative to the reference.

The appropriate conclusion is that the current synthetic generator produces a stable separation between the severity-only formulation and the other formulations. It is not evidence that this separation would necessarily persist in real incident data.

## 4. Patrol results

The patrol experiment demonstrates how different priority orders can propagate into a downstream routing procedure.

For the canonical scenario, the frequency-based, frequency-severity, and current-severity-recency models select the same priority pattern and therefore produce the same baseline and optimized distances. The severity-only model selects a different set/order of priority zones and produces a different route distance.

The 2-opt comparison reduces the route from 23.62 km to 22.31 km for three models in the canonical scenario. For severity-only, the tested route is already unchanged by the 2-opt procedure at the reported precision.

This should be described as a property of the chosen routing heuristic and synthetic coordinates, not as evidence of actual patrol efficiency.

## 5. Research question mapping

### RQ1 — Risk modelling

The models produce materially different outputs when their formulas emphasize different properties of the synthetic incidents. Severity-only creates the largest observed change in the canonical ranking.

### RQ2 — Resource allocation

Model choice can change the distribution of a fixed 12-officer budget. In the canonical scenario, this occurs for severity-only; the other three models match the frequency-only allocation.

### RQ3 — Patrol routing

Changes in model-generated priorities can change the route generated by the fixed routing procedure. The canonical experiment demonstrates this relationship descriptively.

### RQ4 — Reproducibility

The complete pipeline reproduces deterministic results, and the validation command passes after the research pipeline.

### RQ5 — Robustness across synthetic scenarios

The five-seed experiment shows that the main qualitative separation observed in the canonical scenario persists under the current synthetic generation mechanism, while also revealing some seed sensitivity in severity-only and current-severity-recency outputs.

## 6. What the experiment does not show

The experiment does not determine which risk model should be used by a real police organization.

It does not estimate actual crime probabilities, causal effects, public-safety outcomes, or patrol effectiveness.

It also does not provide statistical evidence about Kathmandu Valley as a population because the observations are generated synthetically.

## 7. Evidence level

The current evidence supports a **computational sensitivity and robustness study**. It is stronger than a single demonstration run because the algorithms are deterministic, the pipeline is reproducible, and five synthetic seeds have been tested.

The next major evidence improvement would come from carefully sourced, legally usable real or validated aggregate data, followed by a preregistered or otherwise clearly specified evaluation design.
